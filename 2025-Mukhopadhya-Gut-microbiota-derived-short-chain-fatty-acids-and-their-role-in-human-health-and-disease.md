# Gut microbiota-derived short-chain fatty acids and their role in human health and disease

## Bibliographic info
- Year: 2025
- Authors: Indrani Mukhopadhya; Petra Louis
- Venue: Nature Reviews Microbiology, Volume 23, October 2025, pp. 635-651
- DOI/URL: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41579-025-01183-w

## Main idea
Gut microbiota-derived SCFAs are central host-microbe signaling and metabolic mediators. Their production depends on dietary substrates, microbial cross-feeding networks, and gut environmental factors; their host effects are mediated by transporters, GPCR signaling (for example FFAR2/FFAR3), and epigenetic regulation (including HDAC inhibition). Together, these pathways shape gut homeostasis and influence intestinal and extraintestinal disease risk and progression.

## Main novelty
This review integrates three layers into one mechanistic framework: (1) updated microbial pathway ecology for SCFA generation (including cross-feeding and environmental modulation), (2) molecular host interaction mechanisms linking local and systemic effects, and (3) a translational synthesis of intervention evidence (direct SCFA delivery, encapsulation strategies, acylated starch carriers, fibre/prebiotic modulation), highlighting why observed clinical effects are heterogeneous.

## Experimental procedure
1. Define SCFA production biology by mapping substrate inputs (dietary fibre and host-derived compounds), microbial catabolic pathways, and cross-feeding interactions.
2. Characterize gut environmental modulators (for example pH and hydrogen pressure) that shift pathway flux and SCFA profiles.
3. Trace host exposure and signaling by summarizing absorption/transport and receptor/epigenetic pathways (GPCR signaling, HDAC inhibition, downstream immune and barrier effects).
4. Compare evidence across disease domains (IBD, colorectal cancer, cardio-metabolic, respiratory, neurological conditions) using human observational studies, trials, and animal models.
5. Evaluate therapeutic strategies by comparing delivery modes (enema/oral/microencapsulated/acylated carriers) and dietary modulation approaches (fibre/prebiotics/probiotics), and interpret variability in outcomes.

## Evidence supporting the main idea
- Evidence: SCFA depletion is linked to inflammatory intestinal disease states.
  - Experiment/setup: Meta-analysis of 11 studies in IBD plus disease-cohort microbial functional comparisons.
  - Result (with numbers when available): IBD patients had significantly reduced total faecal SCFAs (including acetate and propionate) and reduced butyrate-production capacity markers.
  - Why it supports the claim: Connects microbiota metabolic output deficits to gut inflammatory pathology and supports SCFAs as functional mediators, not just bystanders.
- Evidence: Mechanistic plausibility from molecular and physiological pathways.
  - Experiment/setup: Integrated transporter, receptor, and epigenetic evidence (MCT1/SMCT1 transport; FFAR2/FFAR3/GPR109A signaling; class I/II HDAC inhibition).
  - Result (with numbers when available): Colonic total SCFAs around 100 mM with approximate acetate:propionate:butyrate ratio 3:1:1; >90% uptake in the large intestine.
  - Why it supports the claim: Demonstrates biologically credible exposure and signaling ranges sufficient to influence local and systemic host processes.
- Evidence: Clinical intervention data show condition-dependent benefits and limitations.
  - Experiment/setup: Table 2 summary of human SCFA trials across GI and metabolic diseases.
  - Result (with numbers when available): Mixed outcomes, including symptom/histology improvements in some ulcerative colitis studies, but null effects in others; in paediatric obesity, oral butyrate reduced BMI and metabolic parameters; in type 2 diabetes, oral butyrate showed no HOMA-IR improvement.
  - Why it supports the claim: Confirms SCFAs can be therapeutically active, while also showing strong context dependence in efficacy.
- Evidence: Population-scale and cohort links support relevance beyond the gut.
  - Experiment/setup: EPIC cohort and PASTURE birth cohort associations.
  - Result (with numbers when available): EPIC reported 25% lower colorectal cancer incidence in highest vs lowest fibre intake quintile (>500,000 participants); in PASTURE (~300 children), higher early-life butyrate associated with lower asthma likelihood.
  - Why it supports the claim: Supports generalizability of SCFA-related pathways to cancer and immune-respiratory outcomes in humans.
- Evidence: Convergent animal-model results provide causal direction.
  - Experiment/setup: Murine studies in allergic airway disease, myocardial infarction, and neuroinflammatory models with SCFA supplementation/depletion.
  - Result (with numbers when available): SCFA feeding attenuated allergic airway inflammation; depletion of butyrate producers worsened airway disease but was reversible by SCFA administration; SCFA supplementation improved selected neurological/cardiac outcomes in model systems.
  - Why it supports the claim: Provides intervention-based causal support for mechanisms that are associative in many human datasets.

## Limitations and caveats
- This is a review article, not a single controlled experiment; conclusions inherit heterogeneity and bias from included studies.
- Many clinical trials are small and differ in SCFA formulation, dose, route, and treatment duration, limiting direct comparability.
- Faecal SCFA concentrations are imperfect proxies for production because SCFAs are extensively absorbed in the colon.
- Species differences (human vs mouse receptor pharmacology and metabolism) limit direct translational certainty.
- Therapeutic response likely depends on baseline microbiome structure, host metabolic state, and intestinal inflammatory context.
