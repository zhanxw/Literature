## Keywords

Gut microbiota; colonization resistance; Enterobacteriaceae; Klebsiella pneumoniae; Escherichia coli; live biotherapeutic products; gluconate competition; inflammatory bowel disease; gnotobiotic mice.

## Main Idea

The authors developed F18-mix, a defined consortium of 18 commensal bacterial strains isolated from a healthy human donor. In gnotobiotic mouse models, F18-mix selectively decolonized intestinal Enterobacteriaceae—including antibiotic-resistant *Klebsiella pneumoniae* and *Escherichia coli*—without broadly disrupting resident commensals. The principal mechanism was ecological control: F18-mix members cooperatively consumed intestinal gluconate, depriving *Klebsiella* of a preferred carbon source and restoring colonization resistance.

## Evidence Supporting the Main Idea

- Faecal microbiota from each of five healthy Japanese donors reduced *K. pneumoniae* Kp-2H7 by approximately 3–4 logs in germ-free C57BL/6 mice (Fig. 1a).
- Screening 31 isolates from the most effective donor produced F18-mix. The complete 18-strain consortium reduced Kp-2H7 as effectively as the parental 31-strain mixture; a 13-strain excluded fraction was much less effective (Fig. 1c,e).
- Removing any of four phylogenetic groups from F18-mix reduced activity, indicating cooperative function rather than a single dominant strain (Fig. 1f).
- F18-mix produced approximately 3–4-log reductions of carbapenemase-producing *K. pneumoniae*, ESBL-producing *E. coli*, *K. aerogenes*, and adherent-invasive *E. coli* in gnotobiotic mice (Fig. 2a). Other tested Pseudomonadota, including *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*, were comparatively resistant.
- In mice carrying Crohn’s disease- or ulcerative-colitis-associated microbiota, F18 strains engrafted after vancomycin treatment and suppressed *K. pneumoniae* or ESBL-positive *E. coli* (Fig. 2b,c; Extended Data Fig. 4a,b).
- In IL-10-deficient colitis models, F18-mix reduced pathogen abundance, histological colitis, faecal lipocalin-2 and calprotectin, and TH1-cell frequency; the less effective F13-mix did not provide the same protection (Extended Data Fig. 4c–g).
- A saturated transposon library of approximately 8 × 10^5 Kp-2H7 mutants identified gluconate and carbohydrate metabolism as major determinants of susceptibility to F18-mix (Fig. 3a–c).
- F18-mix suppressed *Klebsiella* gluconate-utilization genes, reduced faecal gluconate by approximately 2 logs, and lost efficacy when excess gluconate was supplied. A gluconate-deficient diet enhanced decolonization (Fig. 3e–h; Extended Data Fig. 9).
- Eight F18 strains consumed gluconate in vitro and carried alternative gluconate-utilization genes, including gluconate dehydratase pathways. In paediatric ulcerative-colitis samples, gluconate-kinase-bearing Enterobacteriaceae were enriched in active disease, whereas commensals carrying the alternative pathway were associated with inactive disease (Fig. 4; Extended Data Fig. 12).

## Main Novelty

This study converts a donor-derived microbiota effect into a reproducible, defined 18-strain consortium and links its anti-pathobiont activity to a specific ecological resource: intestinal gluconate. It demonstrates that a multi-strain live biotherapeutic can preferentially target Enterobacteriaceae while preserving much of the surrounding microbial community, and provides human-cohort evidence connecting gluconate metabolism with IBD-associated dysbiosis.

## Datasets Used for Evaluation

- **Healthy donor faecal samples:** Samples from five healthy Japanese adults (median age 33 years; range 30–41) were used for FMT comparison and strain isolation. The study recovered 31 unique strains from donor F, 41 from donor I, and 46 from donor K; the resulting culture collection contained 101 isolates used for genomic and gluconate-utilization analyses.
- **Gnotobiotic mouse experiments:** Germ-free C57BL/6 and IL-10-deficient mice were colonized with Kp-2H7, resistant *Klebsiella* or *E. coli* strains, defined consortia, or patient-derived microbiota. Group sizes varied by experiment, generally n = 3–10; these experiments measured faecal CFUs, community engraftment, inflammation, metabolites, transcriptomes, and mutant fitness.
- **PROTECT paediatric ulcerative-colitis cohort:** 94 subjects and 240 samples were analysed. Samples represented inactive disease (n = 119), mild disease (n = 64), and moderate/severe disease (n = 57); a subset of 84 samples was used for gluconate–calprotectin correlation. The cohort tested associations between faecal gluconate, inflammation, and species carrying gluconate pathways.
- **HMP2 adult IBD cohort:** 91 subjects and 1,878 samples were analysed: Crohn’s disease (n = 41 subjects), ulcerative colitis (n = 26), and non-IBD controls (n = 24). The cohort tested whether IBD-associated species and gluconate-pathway gene combinations were reproducibly associated with faecal gluconate after accounting for calprotectin and subject identity.

## Experimental Procedure

- Colonize germ-free mice with Kp-2H7 and administer stool from five healthy donors; longitudinally quantify *Klebsiella* CFUs.
- Culture donor F, I and K stools, identify isolates by 16S rRNA sequencing and whole-genome sequencing, and screen defined isolate mixtures in mice.
- Use antibiotic perturbation and abundance trajectories to down-select the 18-strain F18-mix; test deletion derivatives to assess cooperation among phylogenetic groups.
- Challenge F18-mix with resistant and IBD-associated *Klebsiella* and *E. coli* strains, and measure pathogen burden, strain engraftment and effects on other commensals.
- Test protection from Kp-2H7- or ESBL-*E. coli*-driven colitis in IL-10-deficient mice using histology, lipocalin-2, calprotectin and TH1-cell measurements.
- Compare effective and ineffective consortia using anaerobic co-culture, metabolomics, pathogen transcriptomics, a high-density Kp-2H7 transposon library, and targeted gene deletions.
- Measure faecal carbohydrates by LC–MS/MS, manipulate dietary gluconate and other carbohydrates, and quantify the effect on F18-mix-mediated decolonization.
- Sequence F18 genomes and analyse gluconate-pathway gene carriage, species abundance and disease associations in the PROTECT and HMP2 cohorts.

## Key Biology Insights

- Colonization resistance can be engineered as an ecological property of a defined community rather than as a conventional antibiotic effect.
- *Klebsiella* relies strongly on gluconate in the gut; F18-mix reduces this resource through cooperative consumption, forcing the pathogen into less favourable metabolic conditions.
- The complete consortium is more effective than reduced subsets, suggesting complementary nutrient-use programs and niche coverage across strains.
- F18-mix activity is context-dependent: dietary carbohydrates, intestinal location, existing microbiota and pathogen metabolism can alter efficacy.
- Classical gluconate kinase pathways are associated with Enterobacteriaceae expansion in IBD, whereas alternative gluconate dehydratase pathways in commensals are associated with healthier or inactive-disease states.

## Implications

F18-mix is a candidate live biotherapeutic strategy for reducing intestinal reservoirs of antimicrobial-resistant Enterobacteriaceae and potentially limiting pathogen-driven inflammation. The defined composition improves reproducibility and safety assessment relative to faecal microbiota transplantation. However, the evidence is preclinical: efficacy was established primarily in gnotobiotic mice, the consortium was derived from a small donor set, and dietary and host-context effects may complicate clinical translation. Human trials will need to establish engraftment, dosing, safety, durability, pathogen specificity and performance across diverse diets and microbiomes.
