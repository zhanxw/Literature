## Main Idea

The study explains why the IS110-family bridge recombinase IS621 naturally favors DNA insertion over excision. The recombinase–bridge RNA (bRNA) complex adopts different DNA-recognition geometries in the two reactions: insertion substrates form a bent U-shaped arrangement, whereas excision substrates bind across both bRNA binding loops in a linear, X-shaped arrangement. The latter geometry makes top-strand exchange less efficient, providing a structural basis for directional bias in the transposition cycle.

## Evidence Supporting the Main Idea

- The authors report that bRNA is weakly expressed from IS621 loci in the *Escherichia coli* genome.
- Biochemical measurements described in the abstract show that the IS621 recombinase–bRNA complex mediates excision less efficiently than insertion.
- Cryo-electron microscopy structures of the complex bound to excision DNA substrates reveal two recombinase dimers, with each dimer accommodating one target- or donor-binding loop of the bRNA.
- Excision DNA adopts a linear, X-shaped arrangement across both bRNA loops, unlike the bent U-shaped donor/target arrangement previously reported for insertion.
- The authors link this excision geometry to reduced top-strand exchange efficiency and therefore to the observed insertion-over-excision bias.
- In both reactions, efficiency is modulated by base pairing between bRNA dinucleotides (“handshake guides”) and the top strands of the DNA substrates.

## Main Novelty

This work provides a structural explanation for the directionality of bridge recombination by extending analysis from the previously characterized insertion complex to the excision complex. It connects a distinct substrate geometry with the kinetic asymmetry of the complete IS621 transposition cycle and identifies handshake-guide pairing as a shared control point for both reaction directions.

- Examine bRNA expression from IS621 loci in the *E. coli* genome.
- Reconstitute IS621 recombinase–bRNA complexes with excision DNA substrates.
- Compare excision and insertion efficiencies of the recombinase–bRNA system.
- Determine cryo-electron microscopy structures of the excision complexes.
- Compare DNA recognition and substrate geometry between excision and the previously reported insertion complex.
- Analyze how top-strand exchange and bRNA “handshake-guide” base pairing affect reaction efficiency.

## Key Biology Insights

- IS621 bridge recombination is not directionally symmetric: insertion is favored over excision.
- The directionality is associated with how DNA substrates are positioned across the bRNA loops, rather than with a wholly different recombinase architecture.
- Excision uses an X-shaped, linear substrate arrangement that is unfavorable for top-strand exchange.
- bRNA handshake guides provide a common sequence-recognition mechanism that tunes both insertion and excision.
- The findings complete an important mechanistic gap in the IS110 transposition cycle: how genomic elements are excised to generate circular intermediates.

## Implications

The structural and mechanistic framework can guide engineering of programmable bridge-editing systems. In particular, optimizing bRNA handshake-guide pairing and substrate geometry may improve reaction efficiency or help tune insertion/excision balance. Because the accessible source did not provide the full experimental dataset, the extent to which these principles generalize across IS110 elements or function in cellular editing applications remains to be established.
