## Main Idea
This multinational phase 3 randomized trial tested whether single-dose oral zoliflodacin is non-inferior to ceftriaxone plus azithromycin for microbiological cure of uncomplicated urogenital gonorrhoea.

## Evidence Supporting the Main Idea
- Text evidence: 930 participants were randomized 2:1 (621 zoliflodacin; 309 comparator) across 17 outpatient clinics in five countries (Nov 6, 2019 to March 16, 2023).
- Text evidence: In the microbiological intention-to-treat urogenital population, cure was 90.9% (460/506; 95% CI 88.1-93.3) with zoliflodacin vs 96.2% (229/238; 92.9-98.3) with comparator.
- Text evidence: Treatment difference (comparator minus zoliflodacin) was 5.3% (95% CI 1.4-8.6), below the prespecified 12% non-inferiority margin.
- Text evidence: Safety was comparable, with mostly mild/moderate events and no serious adverse events reported.
- Figure evidence (Figure 1): Trial profile documents participant flow from 1,011 screened to randomized and analysis populations, supporting internal validity of endpoint denominators.
- Table/Figure evidence (Table 3; Figure 2): Secondary analyses at pharyngeal and rectal sites showed similar efficacy patterns between groups (for example, rectal microbiological intention-to-treat cure 87.3% vs 88.6%), though the trial was not powered for non-inferiority at these sites.

## Main Novelty
This is pivotal phase 3 evidence that a first-in-class oral spiropyrimidinetrione can meet non-inferiority criteria against guideline-standard injectable-plus-oral therapy for urogenital gonorrhoea.

## Datasets Used for Evaluation
- Dataset name: Zoliflodacin Phase 3 RCT clinical dataset.
- Main content: Participant-level randomized treatment data, baseline microbiology, test-of-cure culture outcomes, and safety events.
- Sample size:
  - 1,011 screened.
  - 930 randomized (621 zoliflodacin; 309 comparator).
  - Primary endpoint set: microbiological intention-to-treat urogenital population (506 zoliflodacin; 238 comparator).
  - Secondary endpoint sets included rectal and pharyngeal populations.

## Experimental Procedure
- Enrolled participants aged >=12 years with suspected uncomplicated urogenital gonorrhoea at 17 international clinics.
- Randomized participants (2:1) to oral zoliflodacin 3 g or ceftriaxone 500 mg intramuscular plus azithromycin 1 g oral.
- Performed baseline and test-of-cure culture-based microbiological assessments (TOC day 6 +/- 2).
- Evaluated primary endpoint in microbiological intention-to-treat urogenital population using a prespecified non-inferiority margin of 12%.
- Conducted secondary site-specific analyses (rectal/pharyngeal) and comprehensive safety monitoring.

## Key Biology Insights
- Oral zoliflodacin achieved high cure rates in urogenital infection and showed favorable activity patterns at extragenital sites.
- No major post-treatment MIC shift signal was reported in trial summaries, supporting short-term microbiological robustness.
- The mechanism (GyrB-targeting first-in-class agent) offers mechanistic diversity relative to existing first-line therapy components.

## Implications
- Zoliflodacin is a strong candidate for expanding gonorrhoea treatment options, especially where oral therapy access matters.
- Implementation should be coupled with resistance surveillance to preserve efficacy.
- Guideline updates may consider zoliflodacin as an alternative regimen after regulatory and policy review.
