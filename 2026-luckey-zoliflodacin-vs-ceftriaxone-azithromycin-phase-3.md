# Paper Summary

## Keywords
gonorrhoea, zoliflodacin, ceftriaxone, azithromycin, non-inferiority trial, phase 3, randomized controlled trial, safety

## Main Idea
This international phase 3 randomized non-inferiority trial evaluates whether single-dose oral zoliflodacin (3 g) is an effective and safe alternative to ceftriaxone plus azithromycin for uncomplicated urogenital gonorrhoea.

## Evidence Supporting the Main Idea
From 1,011 screened patients, 930 were randomized (621 zoliflodacin, 309 comparator) across 17 outpatient clinics in Belgium, the Netherlands, South Africa, Thailand, and the USA (Nov 6, 2019 to Mar 16, 2023). In the microbiological intention-to-treat urogenital population, cure at test-of-cure (day 6 +/- 2) was 90.9% (460/506; 95% CI 88.1-93.3) with zoliflodacin versus 96.2% (229/238; 92.9-98.3) with comparator. The estimated difference (comparator minus zoliflodacin) was 5.3% (95% CI 1.4-8.6), with upper CI below the prespecified 12% non-inferiority margin. Safety was comparable: common events in zoliflodacin arm were headache (10%), neutropenia (7%), leukopenia (4%); comparator arm had injection-site pain (12%), neutropenia (8%), diarrhea (7%). No serious adverse events were reported.

## Main Novelty
The trial provides pivotal phase 3 evidence that a new oral first-in-class spiropyrimidinetrione (zoliflodacin) can meet non-inferiority criteria versus standard injectable-plus-oral therapy for urogenital gonorrhoea.

## Datasets Used for Evaluation
Prospective trial dataset from a multinational phase 3 RCT:
- 930 randomized participants (microbiological intention-to-treat subset used for primary efficacy endpoint).
- Microbiological cure outcomes from urethral/endocervical culture at test-of-cure.
- Treatment-emergent safety data from both trial arms.
No external benchmark dataset is specified for primary endpoint evaluation.

## Experimental Procedure
Participants aged 12 years and older with suspected uncomplicated urogenital gonorrhoea were randomized 2:1 to oral zoliflodacin 3 g or ceftriaxone 500 mg intramuscular plus azithromycin 1 g oral in an open-label design with masked microbiology staff. The primary endpoint was microbiological cure at day 6 +/- 2 in the microbiological intention-to-treat population. Non-inferiority was predefined using a 12% margin on the two-sided 95% CI for treatment difference, and adverse events were monitored through standard clinical-trial safety assessment.
