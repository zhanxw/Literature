# OmpK26, a Novel Porin Associated with Carbapenem Resistance in Klebsiella pneumoniae

**Authors:** Laura García-Sureda, Antonio Doménech-Sánchez, Mariette Barbier, Carlos Juan, Joan Gascó, Sebastián Albertí

**Journal:** Antimicrobial Agents and Chemotherapy, Vol. 55, Issue 10, pp. 4742-4747 (2011)

**DOI:** [10.1128/aac.00309-11](https://doi.org/10.1128/aac.00309-11)

---

## Main Idea

This study identifies **OmpK26**, a novel outer membrane porin in *Klebsiella pneumoniae* that enables carbapenem resistance by compensating for the loss of the major nonspecific porins OmpK35 and OmpK36. While OmpK26 expression allows the bacteria to survive in the presence of carbapenems, it comes at a significant fitness cost—reducing both in vitro growth and virulence in murine infection models.

## Novelty

1. **Discovery of OmpK26**: First characterization of a 26-kDa oligogalacturonate-specific porin belonging to the KdgM family in *K. pneumoniae*
2. **Compensatory Mechanism**: Demonstrated that porin loss (OmpK35/36) can be compensated by alternative porin expression rather than solely relying on β-lactamase production
3. **Fitness-Virulence Trade-off**: Showed that carbapenem resistance via OmpK26 imposes measurable fitness costs—a concept relevant to understanding the evolutionary dynamics of resistant pathogens
4. **Genetic Link**: Identified *yjhA* as the gene encoding OmpK26

## Datasets Used

The study analyzed clinical isolates from Hospital Universitario Son Espases (Palma de Mallorca, Spain):

**Animal Model**: Murine model of acute systemic infection (CD-1 mice)

## Experimental Procedure

### 1. Isolate Characterization
- **Outer Membrane Protein (OMP) Analysis**: Compared OMP profiles between isogenic susceptible and resistant isolates using SDS-PAGE
- **Mass Spectrometry**: Identified the novel 26-kDa protein as OmpK26
- **DNA Sequencing**: Confirmed *yjhA* as the OmpK26 coding gene and identified mutations in *ompK35* and *ompK36*

### 2. Genetic Manipulation
- **Mutagenesis**: Used insertion-duplication mutagenesis to disrupt *yjhA* in the carbapenem-resistant isolate KpCR-1
- **Complementation Analysis**: Confirmed that loss of OmpK26 leads to re-expression of OmpK36 and reversion to carbapenem susceptibility

### 3. Phenotypic Characterization
- **Antibiotic Susceptibility Testing**: Determined MIC values for carbapenems and other β-lactams
- **Growth Kinetics**: Measured in vitro growth rates and competition experiments between resistant and susceptible strains
- **Virulence Assessment**: Evaluated bacterial load and survival kinetics in a murine acute systemic infection model

### 4. Biochemical Analysis
- **Porin Characterization**: Determined that OmpK26 is a monomeric porin (~330 amino acids)
- **Homology Analysis**: Identified OmpK26 as a member of the KdgM family of oligogalacturonate-specific porins

## Key Findings

1. **OmpK26 is Indispensable for Carbapenem Resistance**: Mutagenesis of *yjhA* in resistant isolates caused loss of resistance and re-expression of OmpK36
2. **Fitness Cost**: OmpK26-expressing strains showed reduced growth rates in vitro compared to wild-type
3. **Virulence Attenuation**: Mice infected with OmpK26-expressing strains showed delayed bacterial dissemination and reduced virulence
4. **Trade-off**: The compensatory porin allows survival under antibiotic pressure but cannot restore the fitness lost from major porin deficiency

## Significance

This work contributes to understanding the multifaceted mechanisms of carbapenem resistance beyond β-lactamase production. The discovery of OmpK26 highlights the importance of outer membrane permeability changes and provides insights into the evolutionary trade-offs faced by antibiotic-resistant bacteria—knowledge that may inform therapeutic strategies targeting fitness costs to combat resistance.
