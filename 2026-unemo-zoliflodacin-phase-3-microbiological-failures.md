## Main Idea
This retrospective genomic analysis examined protocol-defined microbiological failures in the zoliflodacin phase 3 trial to determine whether failures represented true persistence of the same strain or likely reinfection with a different strain.

## Evidence Supporting the Main Idea
- Text evidence: 960 isolates were analyzed (936 baseline isolates from 763 participants; 24 test-of-cure isolates from 20 participants) from trial sites in Belgium, the Netherlands, South Africa, Thailand, and the USA (Nov 6, 2019 to March 16, 2023).
- Text evidence: Among 22 zoliflodacin failures, MICs remained within the reported wild-type range (<=0.008 to 0.5 mg/L), with no selection of elevated-MIC isolates between baseline and test-of-cure.
- Text evidence: Whole-genome sequencing showed 5/22 failures (23%, 95% CI 10-43) had different baseline vs test-of-cure strains, supporting reinfection in these cases.
- Text evidence: 17/22 failures had indistinguishable baseline and test-of-cure strains; 13/22 total failures (59%, 95% CI 39-77) were urogenital or rectal with generally low MICs (<=0.008 to 0.25 mg/L), leaving no clear microbiological resistance explanation.
- Figure evidence (Figure 1): Baseline MIC distribution in 936 isolates was centered in wild-type values (largest bin at 0.06 mg/L, 367/936), with only 3/936 isolates at 0.5 mg/L.
- Figure evidence (Figure 2): Phylogenomic clustering of paired baseline/test-of-cure isolates showed both patterns: tightly related pairs (same-strain persistence) and clearly separated pairs (different-strain reinfection pattern).

## Main Novelty
The study applies WGS directly to phase 3 gonorrhoea trial failure cases and quantifies how many apparent failures are genetically consistent with reinfection versus same-strain persistence.

## Datasets Used for Evaluation
- Dataset name: Zoliflodacin phase 3 failure-analysis isolate set.
- Main content: Baseline and test-of-cure N. gonorrhoeae isolates with paired phenotypic MIC and genomic data.
- Sample size:
  - 960 total isolates for susceptibility testing.
  - 936 baseline isolates from 763 participants.
  - 24 test-of-cure isolates from 20 participants.
  - WGS on paired failure isolates (44 zoliflodacin-case isolates; 2 comparator-case isolates) plus 3 high-MIC baseline isolates.

## Experimental Procedure
- Conducted a retrospective observational substudy inside the completed international phase 3 randomized trial.
- Performed agar dilution MIC testing for zoliflodacin and comparators on trial isolates under standardized methods.
- Selected protocol-defined microbiological failures and retrieved paired baseline/test-of-cure isolates from the same anatomical sites when available.
- Performed WGS and compared relatedness (SNP-based analyses, typing schemes) and resistance-associated loci including gyrB.
- Integrated microbiology plus genomics to classify cases as likely reinfection (different strain) or same-strain persistence.

## Key Biology Insights
- No sequenced isolate showed mutations known to drive elevated zoliflodacin MIC in this dataset, despite observed clinical failures.
- A substantial subset of failures likely reflects epidemiologic reinfection rather than pharmacologic failure.
- Pharyngeal failures included cases with MICs toward the upper wild-type range, consistent with known biological/PK challenges at this site.

## Implications
- Trial cure rates can be underestimated if reinfections are not separated from true treatment failures.
- Future gonorrhoea treatment trials should incorporate paired-isolate genomics as standard failure adjudication.
- Post-approval surveillance should combine MIC testing and genomic monitoring to rapidly detect emergence of true zoliflodacin resistance.
