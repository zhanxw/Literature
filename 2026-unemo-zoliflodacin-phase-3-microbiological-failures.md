# Paper Summary

### Authors
- Magnus Unemo et al.

### Journal
- The Lancet Microbe

### Publication Date
- 2026 (published online February 5, 2026)

### DOI
- https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lanmic.2025.101270

## Keywords
- Neisseria gonorrhoeae
- zoliflodacin
- microbiological failure
- whole-genome sequencing
- reinfection
- phase 3 trial

## Main Idea
- This retrospective genomic substudy analyzed protocol-defined microbiological failures from the zoliflodacin phase 3 trial to distinguish true treatment failure from probable reinfection.
- The study combined susceptibility testing and whole-genome sequencing (WGS) of paired baseline and test-of-cure isolates.
- The central conclusion is that many apparent failures were not explained by emerging zoliflodacin resistance.

## Evidence Supporting the Main Idea
- 960 isolates were analyzed (936 baseline isolates from 763 participants, plus 24 test-of-cure isolates from 20 participants).
- In zoliflodacin failures (22 infections in 19 participants), baseline and test-of-cure MICs remained in the wild-type range (<=0.008 to 0.5 mg/L), with no evidence of resistance selection.
- WGS showed 5/22 (23%; 95% CI 10-43) zoliflodacin microbiological failures had different baseline versus test-of-cure strains, consistent with reinfection.
- The remaining 17/22 had indistinguishable baseline and test-of-cure strains; 13/22 total failures (59%; 95% CI 39-77) were urogenital or rectal with generally low MICs and no clear microbiological resistance explanation.
- No sequenced isolates contained known mutations associated with elevated zoliflodacin MICs.
- The single comparator-arm failure also had different strains at baseline versus test of cure.

## Main Novelty
- First use of WGS in this context to adjudicate gonorrhoea phase-3 trial “microbiological failures” at strain level.
- Quantifies the fraction of apparent failures likely reflecting reinfection rather than antimicrobial resistance.
- Provides a template for integrating genomics into endpoint interpretation in future STI treatment trials.

## Datasets Used for Evaluation
- Dataset name: Zoliflodacin phase 3 microbiological-failure isolate dataset.
  - Main content: baseline and test-of-cure N. gonorrhoeae isolates with agar dilution MIC data.
  - Sample size: 960 total isolates; 936 baseline isolates (763 participants); 24 test-of-cure isolates (20 participants).
- WGS subset:
  - Paired isolates from failures (zoliflodacin: 44 isolates from 19 participants; comparator: 2 isolates from 1 participant), plus 3 baseline isolates with highest zoliflodacin MIC.

## Experimental Procedure
- Retrieve baseline and test-of-cure isolates from the completed international phase 3 trial.
- Perform antimicrobial susceptibility testing by standardized agar dilution.
- Select protocol-defined failure cases and compare paired baseline/test-of-cure isolates from the same anatomical sites.
- Conduct WGS on paired isolates and analyze strain relatedness plus resistance-associated loci.
- Integrate phenotypic MIC and genomic evidence to classify likely reinfection versus same-strain persistence.

## Key Biology Insights
- Zoliflodacin target-associated resistance was not selected in sequenced failure isolates.
- A measurable portion of trial-defined failures likely reflects reinfection dynamics.
- For same-strain failures at low MICs, mechanisms beyond straightforward resistance (for example site-specific pharmacodynamic factors) may contribute.

## Implications
- Cure-rate interpretation in gonorrhoea trials should account for reinfection to avoid underestimating treatment efficacy.
- WGS should be incorporated as standard adjudication for future gonorrhoea treatment trials.
- Post-approval surveillance should combine MIC phenotyping and genomics to detect emerging resistance early.
