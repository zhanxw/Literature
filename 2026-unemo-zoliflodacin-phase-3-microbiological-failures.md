# Paper Summary

## Keywords
Neisseria gonorrhoeae, zoliflodacin, phase 3 trial, microbiological failure, whole-genome sequencing, antimicrobial susceptibility, reinfection

## Main Idea
This retrospective genomic follow-up of protocol-defined microbiological failures from the international zoliflodacin phase 3 gonorrhoea trial tests whether apparent failures reflected true treatment failure or reinfection, using paired baseline and test-of-cure isolates with antimicrobial susceptibility testing and whole-genome sequencing.

## Evidence Supporting the Main Idea
The study analyzed 960 isolates (936 baseline from 763 participants and 24 test-of-cure isolates from 20 participants) from 16 clinics in Belgium, the Netherlands, South Africa, Thailand, and the USA (Nov 6, 2019 to Mar 16, 2023). In 22 zoliflodacin microbiological failures, MICs remained in the wild-type range (<=0.008 to 0.5 mg/L) with no resistance selection signal. WGS showed different strains at test-of-cure versus baseline in 5/22 infections (23%, 95% CI 10-43), supporting reinfection rather than drug failure in those cases. The remaining 17/22 failures had indistinguishable strains; 13/22 total failures (59%, 95% CI 39-77) were urogenital/rectal cases with low MICs (<=0.008 to 0.25 mg/L) and no detected gyrB mutations linked to elevated zoliflodacin MIC.

## Main Novelty
This is presented as the first report using WGS to investigate possible microbiological failures in a randomized gonorrhoea treatment trial, quantifying how many failures are likely reinfections versus persistent same-strain infections.

## Datasets Used for Evaluation
Primary trial-derived isolate dataset from the zoliflodacin phase 3 RCT:
- 960 total N. gonorrhoeae isolates for susceptibility testing.
- Paired baseline and test-of-cure isolates from failure cases for WGS (44 zoliflodacin-case isolates, 2 comparator-case isolates), plus 3 baseline high-MIC isolates.
No external public dataset is reported as the main evaluation source.

## Experimental Procedure
The authors performed a retrospective observational analysis of specimens collected during a completed phase 3 RCT. They measured zoliflodacin MICs by agar dilution, then sequenced paired baseline and test-of-cure isolates from protocol-defined microbiological failures and compared genomic relatedness and resistance-associated mutations (including gyrB targets) between timepoints. They integrated MIC and WGS evidence to classify likely reinfection versus persistent same-strain failure patterns.
