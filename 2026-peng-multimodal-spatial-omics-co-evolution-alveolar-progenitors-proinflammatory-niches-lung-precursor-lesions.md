# Paper Summary

### Authors
Fuduan Peng, Ansam Sinjab, Yibo Dai, et al.

### Journal
Cancer Cell

### Publication Date
2026-02-09

### DOI
10.1016/j.ccell.2025.10.004

## Keywords
- Lung adenocarcinoma
- Precursor lesions
- Spatial transcriptomics
- Alveolar progenitors
- IL1B
- Immune interception
- Precancer evolution

## Main Idea
- The paper maps how lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) precursor lesions evolve into invasive disease and argues that this process is shaped by epithelial alveolar progenitors embedded in proinflammatory spatial niches.
- The authors identify KRT8-positive alveolar progenitor-like epithelial states associated with tumor-related meta-programs and enriched IL1B-IL1R1 signaling.
- They propose that these epithelial-inflammatory niches are most prominent in precursor lesions and represent a stage-specific interception target before fully invasive LUAD develops.

## Evidence Supporting the Main Idea
- The human discovery cohort included spatial transcriptomic maps of 56 precursor lesions and LUAD samples from 25 patients, plus an independent cohort of 36 lesions from 19 patients.
- Across the study, the authors analyzed 486,519 spatial spots and 5.4 million cells.
- The lesions spanned the pathological continuum, including 11 atypical adenomatous hyperplasia (AAH), 14 adenocarcinoma in situ (AIS), 4 minimally invasive adenocarcinoma (MIA), and 26 LUAD samples, plus one lesion-adjacent normal sample.
- Spatial analysis showed that precursor lesions and LUADs occupy different region-specific programs, with precursor lesions retaining stronger alveolar-lineage features while LUADs show greater heterogeneity and tumor-associated programs.
- Spatially resolved clonal analysis identified multiple evolutionary patterns, including cases with shared precursor-to-invasive clones and others with non-shared clonal architectures, indicating patient-specific evolutionary routes.
- Single-nucleus RNA-seq identified alveolar lineage states including reactive pneumocyte or KRT8-positive alveolar cells that the paper positions as early LUAD precursors.
- The study reports that these epithelial progenitor-like states sit in niches enriched for proinflammatory cells, particularly IL1B-high macrophages, and that IL1B-IL1R1 interactions are more prominent in precursor lesions than in invasive LUAD.
- Mouse validation showed that analogous inflammatory niches are conserved and promote alveolar progenitor growth.
- In mouse interception experiments, anti-IL-1beta treatment alone or combined with anti-PD-1 reduced alveolar progenitor or tumor-associated epithelial populations during the precancerous phase.

## Main Novelty
- The major novelty is the integration of human spatial transcriptomics, clonal inference, single-nucleus RNA-seq, Xenium-based validation, and mouse intervention experiments into one model of stage-specific LUAD evolution.
- The study moves beyond cataloging premalignant lesions by identifying a specific epithelial-inflammatory niche architecture linked to early disease progression.
- It also provides mechanistic and therapeutic support for targeting inflammation during the precancer stage rather than waiting for invasive cancer.

## Datasets Used for Evaluation
- Human Visium spatial transcriptomics cohort:
- 56 tissue samples from 25 patients spanning AAH, AIS, MIA, and LUAD.
- 486,519 retained spots after quality control.
- Human independent validation cohort:
- 36 lesions from 19 patients on a tissue microarray with 188 tissue cores.
- Human matched bulk genomics:
- 75 matched samples including uninvolved normal lung tissues from 23 of the 25 patients.
- Human single-nucleus RNA-seq:
- 401,635 nuclei after filtering from fixed tissue sections adjacent to spatially profiled sections.
- Additional spatial validation:
- Xenium 5K analysis on selected human lesions and tissue microarrays.
- Mouse models:
- Gprc5a-deficient and Kras-driven LUAD-related models used to validate inflammatory niches and test anti-IL-1beta and anti-PD-1 interception strategies.

## Experimental Procedure
- Profile human precursor lesions and LUADs with Visium CytAssist spatial transcriptomics and perform detailed pathology-guided annotation.
- Infer copy number alteration patterns from epithelial spatial spots to reconstruct precursor-to-invasive clonal relationships.
- Perform single-nucleus RNA-seq on adjacent sections to resolve epithelial and immune cell states.
- Integrate spatial and single-nucleus data to identify alveolar progenitor-like states and their associated meta-programs.
- Map ligand-receptor interactions, especially IL1B-IL1R1 signaling, between epithelial and myeloid compartments.
- Validate niche structure in additional human samples with Xenium-based spatial profiling.
- Test whether inflammatory signaling supports lesion progression in mouse models and whether blocking IL-1beta alone or with PD-1 inhibition reduces precursor-associated epithelial states.

## Key Biology Insights
- Early LUAD development appears to involve KRT8-positive alveolar progenitor-like epithelial states rather than a direct, uniform jump from normal alveolar cells to invasive tumor.
- These epithelial states are supported by proinflammatory macrophage-rich niches, especially those involving IL1B signaling.
- The relevant inflammatory niche is more characteristic of precursor lesions than established LUAD, suggesting a narrow stage-specific window for intervention.
- Evolution from precursor to invasive disease is heterogeneous across patients, but the epithelial-inflammatory niche logic is recurrent enough to be therapeutically relevant.

## Implications
- Spatial profiling of precursor lung lesions could help identify patients whose lesions are progressing along an inflammatory, targetable trajectory.
- IL-1beta-centered interception strategies may be more effective in premalignant or very early-stage settings than in advanced LUAD.
- The study provides a framework for treating precancer progression as a spatial ecosystem problem rather than only a mutation-acquisition problem.
