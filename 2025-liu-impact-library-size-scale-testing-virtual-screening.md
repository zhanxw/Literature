# Paper Summary

### Authors
- Fangyu Liu et al.

### Journal
- Nature Chemical Biology

### Publication Date
- 2025

### DOI
- https://doi.org/10.1038/s41589-024-01797-w

## Keywords
- virtual screening
- docking
- library scale
- hit rate
- AmpC beta-lactamase

## Main Idea
- The paper quantifies how larger docking libraries and larger experimental follow-up improve virtual-screening outcomes.

## Evidence Supporting the Main Idea
- Compared 1.7 billion-molecule screen versus prior 99 million-molecule campaign against AmpC.
- Tested 1,521 synthesized compounds in the large campaign versus 44 previously.
- Observed about 2x higher hit rate, improved potency/scaffold diversity, and roughly 50x more inhibitors identified.
- Hit rates from subsampling stabilized only when testing several hundred compounds.

## Main Novelty
- Controlled, side-by-side empirical test of both library size and testing scale effects in structure-based screening.

## Datasets Used for Evaluation
- Virtual library: ~1.7 billion molecules (large) and 99 million (comparison).
- Experimental validation: 1,521 compounds (large) and 44 compounds (prior campaign).

## Experimental Procedure
- Dock ultra-large libraries to AmpC beta-lactamase.
- Select compounds by docking score and synthesize/test in vitro.
- Compare hit rate, potency, and chemotype diversity to smaller-screen baseline.
- Perform subsampling analyses to assess statistical stability of hit-rate estimates.

## Key Biology Insights
- Large chemical spaces contain substantially more tractable ligands than typical follow-up campaigns currently test.

## Implications
- Virtual screening programs benefit from scaling both in silico libraries and wet-lab validation depth.
