# Paper Summary

## Keywords
Neisseria gonorrhoeae, zoliflodacin, antimicrobial resistance surveillance, WHO EGASP, gyrB mutation, agar dilution, global health

## Main Idea
The paper reports contemporary international in vitro susceptibility of N. gonorrhoeae to zoliflodacin across WHO EGASP sentinel countries (2021-2024), to support clinical introduction while identifying early resistance signals.

## Evidence Supporting the Main Idea
The study tested 2,993 clinical isolates from eight countries across three WHO regions (Southeast Asia, Western Pacific, Africa). Zoliflodacin MICs ranged from 0.001 to 1 mg/L with a modal MIC of 0.032 mg/L, indicating a predominantly wild-type distribution. Only two isolates (both from Cambodia) showed higher MICs (0.5 and 1 mg/L), and both carried the GyrB D429N mutation associated with elevated zoliflodacin MICs. The authors also report activity against ceftriaxone- and azithromycin-resistant gonococcal strains.

## Main Novelty
This work provides one of the largest recent multicountry WHO EGASP susceptibility snapshots for zoliflodacin (n=2,993), with linked molecular evidence (gyrB screening) identifying rare high-MIC outliers during pre-rollout surveillance.

## Datasets Used for Evaluation
WHO EGASP isolate collection (2021-2024):
- Cambodia (482), Indonesia (101), Malawi (121), Philippines (843), South Africa (597), Thailand (250), Uganda (350), Viet Nam (249).
- Total: 2,993 male urethral-discharge-derived clinical N. gonorrhoeae isolates.
No randomized intervention dataset was used.

## Experimental Procedure
Clinical isolates were collected through WHO EGASP sentinel surveillance and shipped to the WHO Collaborating Centre in Orebro, Sweden. The team cultured isolates under standardized conditions and measured zoliﬂodacin MICs by agar dilution following CLSI guidance with WHO reference strains for quality control. They then used Illumina WGS to inspect gyrB resistance-relevant codons (including D429, K450, S467) and interpreted phenotypic-molecular concordance for surveillance conclusions.
