## Main Idea
- This WHO EGASP surveillance study measured contemporary in vitro zoliflodacin susceptibility across multiple countries (2021-2024) to establish baseline global activity and identify early molecular resistance signals before broad clinical rollout.
- Not specified in paper

## Evidence Supporting the Main Idea
- Text evidence: 2,993 clinical isolates from eight countries across WHO Southeast Asia, Western Pacific, and African regions were tested by agar dilution.
- Text evidence: Overall MIC range was 0.001 to 1 mg/L, with modal MIC 0.032 mg/L, MIC50 0.032 mg/L, and MIC90 0.064 mg/L, consistent with high susceptibility.
- Text evidence: Only two isolates (Cambodia, 2022) showed higher MICs (0.5 and 1 mg/L), and both carried GyrB D429N.
- Text evidence: GyrB S467N was found in 14 isolates (0.5%) across several countries.
- Figure evidence (Figure 1): The full 2021-2024 MIC distribution (n=2,993) is predominantly wild-type, with a sparse right tail and rare high-MIC outliers.
- Table evidence (Table 1): Country-level yearly MIC summaries show broadly similar susceptibility across regions, with limited local shifts (for example, slightly higher distribution in Vietnam).

## Datasets Used for Evaluation
- Dataset name: WHO EGASP gonococcal isolate surveillance dataset (2021-2024).
- Main content: Male urethral-discharge-derived N. gonorrhoeae isolates with phenotypic MICs and gyrB sequence features.
- Sample size:
- Cambodia: 482 isolates.
- Indonesia: 101 isolates.
- Malawi: 121 isolates.
- Philippines: 843 isolates.
- South Africa: 597 isolates.
- Thailand: 250 isolates.
- Uganda: 350 isolates.
- Viet Nam: 249 isolates.
- Total: 2,993 isolates.

## Experimental Procedure
- Collected gonococcal isolates through standardized WHO EGASP sentinel surveillance in participating countries.
- Performed zoliflodacin MIC testing by agar dilution under CLSI-referenced protocols with WHO reference strains for quality control.
- Sequenced isolates (Illumina) and screened key gyrB codons (D429, K450, S467) associated with reduced susceptibility.
- Compared MIC distributions by year and country and interpreted rare high-MIC isolates with matched genotype data.
- Not specified in paper

## Key Biology Insights
- Zoliflodacin retained strong in vitro activity across geographically diverse recent isolates.
- The D429N substitution in GyrB remains a key marker linked to elevated MIC outliers.
- Rare target-gene variants can be present before large-scale drug deployment, supporting proactive genomic surveillance.
- Not specified in paper

## Implications
- The data support cautious clinical introduction of zoliflodacin with concurrent resistance monitoring.
- WHO EGASP can function as an early-warning system for emerging zoliflodacin resistance.
- Countries introducing zoliflodacin should pair routine AST with molecular surveillance of gyrB mutations.
