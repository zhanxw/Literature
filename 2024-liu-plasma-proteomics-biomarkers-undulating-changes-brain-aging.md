# Paper Summary
### Authors
- Wei-Shi Liu et al.

### Journal
- Nature Aging

### Publication Date
- 2024 (accepted October 17, 2024)

### DOI
- 10.1038/s43587-024-00753-6

## Keywords
- Brain aging
- Plasma proteomics
- Biomarkers
- Brain age gap (BAG)
- Mendelian randomization

## Main Idea
- The study identifies plasma protein biomarkers associated with brain aging and characterizes non-linear age-related proteomic change patterns.

## Evidence Supporting the Main Idea
- Brain age gap was estimated using multimodal imaging data from 10,949 healthy adults.
- Proteome-wide association in 4,696 participants across 2,922 proteins identified 13 proteins significantly associated with BAG.
- BCAN (beta = -0.838, P = 2.63e-10) and GDF15 (beta = 0.825, P = 3.48e-11) were highlighted with additional associations to clinical outcomes.
- The paper reports proteomic change peaks around ages 57, 70, and 78.

## Main Novelty
- Joint mapping of plasma proteome signatures and age-dependent inflection points in brain aging at population scale.

## Datasets Used for Evaluation
- Multimodal brain imaging cohort: 10,949 healthy adults.
- Plasma proteomics cohort: 4,696 participants, 2,922 proteins.
- Clinical associations for dementia/stroke/movement outcomes.

## Experimental Procedure
- Train/predict brain age from multimodal MRI and compute BAG.
- Run proteome-wide association analyses between proteins and BAG.
- Analyze temporal age trajectories of protein changes.
- Use Mendelian randomization to test causal support for key proteins.

## Key Biology Insights
- Stress, regeneration, and inflammation-linked proteins are strongly connected to brain aging trajectories.
- BCAN emerges as a strong candidate biomarker with possible causal involvement.

## Implications
- Plasma proteomics may support early risk stratification and intervention for neurodegenerative/cerebrovascular aging.
