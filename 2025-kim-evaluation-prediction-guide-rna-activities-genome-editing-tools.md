## Keywords

## Main Idea
- This review synthesizes high-throughput and machine-learning methods for evaluating and predicting guide RNA activity across genome-editing platforms.

## Evidence Supporting the Main Idea
- Summarizes evidence from large high-throughput datasets measuring editing efficiency and specificity.
- Describes how those datasets support predictive models used to select guide RNAs and editors under varying conditions.
- Reviews progress across nuclease editors (Cas9/Cas12a), base editors, and prime editors, including AI-assisted tool development.

## Main Novelty
- Integrative framework-level review connecting assay design, predictive modeling, and editor evolution across multiple genome-editing modalities.

## Datasets Used for Evaluation
- Aggregated high-throughput genome-editing benchmark datasets reported in prior literature.
- Dataset-level sample sizes: Not specified in this extracted review text.

## Experimental Procedure
- Compare assay paradigms for quantifying on-target activity and off-target effects.
- Analyze modeling approaches for guide-RNA efficacy/specificity prediction.
- Contrast performance and applicability across editor classes and experimental contexts.
- Discuss AI-driven design/evolution strategies for next-generation editors.

## Key Biology Insights
- Guide RNA sequence context and editor-specific constraints are central determinants of editing performance and specificity.

## Implications
- Better predictive design pipelines can improve reproducibility and safety in research and therapeutic genome editing.
