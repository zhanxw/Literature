## Main Idea

This review article examines the role of the gut microbiota in gynaecological cancers (ovarian, endometrial, and cervical cancers). The paper explores how gut microbiota influences cancer development through immune regulation, metabolic pathways, and inflammatory responses, and discusses potential clinical applications of microbiome modulation in precision oncology.

## Main Novelty

The key contributions of this review include:

1. **Comprehensive Mechanistic Framework:** Synthesizes evidence linking gut microbiota composition to reproductive tract cancers through multiple pathways including systemic immune modulation, oestrogen metabolism, and chronic inflammation.

2. **Treatment Response Analysis:** Examines how gut dysbiosis affects chemotherapy sensitivity and immunotherapy responses, with evidence that antibiotic use during cancer treatment correlates with poorer prognosis.

3. **Clinical Translation Insights:** Discusses emerging applications of microbiome-based biomarkers for patient stratification and adjuvant therapeutic interventions (dietary modifications, probiotics, faecal microbiota transplantation).

## Key Findings

### Microbiota Alterations in Gynaecological Cancers
- Patients with ovarian, endometrial, and cervical cancers show distinct gut microbiota alterations
- Characterized by reduced microbial diversity
- Shifts toward taxa associated with dysbiosis and chronic inflammation

### Mechanistic Pathways
- **Immune Regulation:** Gut-derived metabolites and microbial translocation influence systemic immune responses
- **Oestrogen Metabolism:** Microbiota affects oestrogen metabolism, creating a tumour-conducive microenvironment
- **Inflammation:** Dysbiosis fosters chronic inflammation linked to cancer progression

### Treatment Implications
- Dysbiosis reduces chemotherapy sensitivity
- Gut microbiota composition alters immunotherapy responses
- Antibiotic use during treatment linked to worse outcomes
- Microbiome modulation (diet, probiotics, FMT) shows therapeutic potential

## Datasets and Evidence Base

This is a narrative review synthesizing findings from 192 references. The paper discusses heterogeneous evidence across:
- Various study designs (clinical cohorts, preclinical models)
- Different patient populations
- Multiple cancer subtypes (ovarian, endometrial, cervical)

## Limitations and Future Directions

- Most microbiome-based applications remain investigational
- Current evidence limited by heterogeneity in study designs, patient cohorts, and cancer subtypes
- Need for standardized methodologies and larger clinical trials
