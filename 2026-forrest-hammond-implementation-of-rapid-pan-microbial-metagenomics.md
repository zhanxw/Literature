### Authors

### Journal

The Lancet Microbe

### Publication Date

15 October 2026 (available online)

### DOI

[10.1016/j.lanmic.2026.101461](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lanmic.2026.101461)

## Keywords

Paediatric intensive care; respiratory infection; metagenomics; pan-microbial sequencing; antimicrobial stewardship; diagnostic microbiology; clinical implementation.

## Main Idea

The study evaluated whether a previously validated rapid respiratory metagenomics workflow could be implemented in paediatric intensive care and provide clinically useful same-day preliminary and next-day final results. In a single-centre prospective observational pilot, the workflow detected bacteria, fungi, and DNA/RNA viruses with high specificity and good sensitivity, and the results were associated with changes in antimicrobial management in a substantial minority of samples.

## Evidence Supporting the Main Idea

- 177 respiratory samples from 122 patients were tested; 174 (98%) passed quality control.
- At 16 hours of sequencing, sensitivity was 89% (95% CI 80–95) for bacteria, 100% (70–100) for fungi, and 87% (76–93) for viruses. Specificity was at least 99% for each microbial kingdom.
- Metagenomics identified 50 organisms in 42 of 174 samples (24%) that routine diagnostics did not detect; all were confirmed by orthogonal PCR.
- Clinical impact was observed in 51 of 174 samples (29%), including antimicrobial-therapy changes in 46 (26%).
- Among the 33 surveyed ICU staff, 23 (72%) reported a positive view of metagenomics, none reported a negative view, and 22 (67%) perceived additional benefits over routine testing.

## Main Novelty

The paper reports deployment of a rapid, pan-microbial respiratory metagenomics service outside the centre where the protocol was developed, specifically in paediatric intensive care. It also describes adaptations needed for children, including handling polymicrobial upper-respiratory-tract samples, and combines diagnostic-performance, clinical-impact, feasibility, and staff-perspective outcomes.

## Datasets Used for Evaluation

- **Prospective paediatric intensive-care respiratory-sample cohort:** 177 samples from 122 patients treated in a general paediatric ICU or cardiac paediatric ICU at Great Ormond Street Hospital, London, UK, from 4 November 2024 to 28 February 2025. Patients were selected by intensive-care staff because of clinical suspicion of lower-respiratory-tract infection; no exclusion criteria were specified in the abstract. This cohort evaluated feasibility, diagnostic performance, and clinical impact.
- **Concurrent routine-diagnostics comparator:** Separate aliquots from the same samples underwent culture and PCR testing. These results were used to assess metagenomic sensitivity/specificity and to identify organisms missed by routine testing.
- **Clinical-record review:** Electronic health records were reviewed to determine changes in antimicrobials, diagnosis, or infection-control management attributable in practice to the metagenomic result.
- **Staff questionnaire:** An anonymous online semistructured questionnaire was completed by 33 ICU staff members to assess perceived value and acceptability.

## Experimental Procedure

- Enrol respiratory samples from patients in the two paediatric ICUs when clinicians suspected lower-respiratory-tract infection.
- Apply a published rapid metagenomic workflow that simultaneously assays bacteria, fungi, and DNA and RNA viruses.
- Return preliminary results on the same day and final results the following day; the reported performance checkpoint was 16 hours of sequencing.
- Send separate sample aliquots for concurrent routine culture and PCR testing.
- Compare metagenomic calls with routine diagnostics and confirm metagenomics-only organism detections with orthogonal PCR.
- Review electronic health records for antimicrobial, diagnostic, and infection-control changes.
- Survey ICU staff anonymously about their experience and perceived benefit.
- Adapt the adult-developed protocol for paediatric samples, particularly the greater frequency of polymicrobial upper-respiratory-tract material.

## Key Biology Insights

- Rapid pan-microbial sequencing can detect bacterial, fungal, and viral respiratory pathogens in a paediatric ICU sample stream with very high specificity.
- The workflow recovered clinically relevant organisms not detected by routine testing, although the study does not establish that every additional detection represented causative infection.
- Paediatric respiratory samples create a biological interpretation challenge because polymicrobial upper-respiratory-tract colonisation or contamination is more common; this required protocol adaptation.
- The authors identify antimicrobial-resistance prediction and further sensitivity gains as biological and technical requirements before broad clinical implementation.

## Implications

Rapid respiratory metagenomics appears feasible as an adjunct to paediatric intensive-care diagnostics and may support antimicrobial stewardship, including stopping, starting, or changing therapy. However, the observational single-centre design cannot isolate the causal contribution of metagenomic results to management decisions. The authors recommend multicentre studies across multiple winter seasons with control groups, predefined patient/sample selection, patient-outcome measures, and cost-effectiveness analyses. The study also cautions that broader adoption will require improved sensitivity and reliable antimicrobial-resistance prediction.
