# Paper Summary
### Authors
- Gauri Kartini Shastry and Daniel L. Tortorice

### Journal
- American Economic Journal: Economic Policy

### Publication Date
- 2025

### DOI
- 10.1257/pol.20230036

## Keywords
- Global health aid
- Vaccination
- Causal inference
- Child mortality
- Gavi

## Main Idea
- The paper estimates the causal impact of Gavi vaccine support on vaccination coverage and child mortality using cross-country variation in rollout timing.

## Evidence Supporting the Main Idea
- Gavi support increased vaccine coverage by about 2-5 percentage points overall.
- Effects were larger (10-20 percentage points) for newer vaccines.
- Related-cause child mortality fell by about 1 per 1,000 live births.
- Authors estimate ~1.5 million lives saved at roughly US$9,000 per life saved.

## Main Novelty
- Quasi-experimental multi-country design linking differential vaccine-support timing to both coverage and mortality outcomes.

## Datasets Used for Evaluation
- Country-by-vaccine panel data on Gavi support timing and vaccine coverage.
- Cause-specific child mortality data in low- and middle-income countries.
- Gavi funding and program data (over US$16B since 1999).

## Experimental Procedure
- Construct panel with staggered introduction of Gavi-supported vaccines.
- Estimate treatment effects exploiting differences across countries and vaccine types.
- Test robustness and heterogeneity across older versus newer vaccines.
- Translate estimated mortality effects into cost-effectiveness calculations.

## Key Biology Insights
- Not a biology-mechanism study; focus is public-health policy and population outcomes.

## Implications
- Provides strong evidence that targeted vaccine aid can be highly cost-effective and mortality-reducing at scale.
