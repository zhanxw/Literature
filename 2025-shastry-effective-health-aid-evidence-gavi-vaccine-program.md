# Paper Summary

### Authors
- Gauri Kartini Shastry
- Daniel L. Tortorice

### Journal
- American Economic Journal: Economic Policy

### Publication Date
- 2025

### DOI
- https://doi.org/10.1257/pol.20230036

## Keywords
- global health aid
- vaccination coverage
- child mortality
- causal inference
- triple-difference design
- Gavi

## Main Idea
- The paper estimates the causal impact of Gavi vaccine support on vaccination coverage and child mortality in low- and middle-income countries.
- Identification uses staggered variation in support timing across countries, vaccines, and cohorts.
- The core claim is that Gavi aid substantially increased vaccine uptake and reduced vaccine-related child mortality at high cost-effectiveness.

## Evidence Supporting the Main Idea
- The paper reports that Gavi support increased vaccine coverage by about 2-5 percentage points across all vaccines.
- Effects were larger for newer vaccines, with estimated increases of about 10-20 percentage points.
- Child mortality from related causes fell by about 1 death per 1,000 live births.
- The authors estimate approximately 1.5-1.6 million lives saved during the study period at around US$9,000 (about US$8,750 in extended calculations) per life saved.
- Results are supported by a triple-difference framework with country-by-vaccine, country-by-cohort, and vaccine-by-cohort fixed effects, plus robustness using methods that address staggered-treatment bias.

## Main Novelty
- Provides a causal, multi-country evaluation of a major global vaccine aid program using within-country and within-vaccine rollout timing variation.
- Distinguishes effects on older vaccines versus newer vaccines (including pneumococcal and rotavirus) where mortality gains are larger.
- Links aid flows to both intermediate outcomes (coverage) and welfare outcomes (cause-specific mortality).

## Datasets Used for Evaluation
- Country-by-vaccine-by-cohort panel for Gavi support timing and vaccine coverage.
  - Main content: vaccine-specific rollout/support timing and coverage outcomes.
  - Sample size: multi-country panel; exact observation count not specified in paper excerpt.
- Cause-specific child mortality data (ages 1-59 months).
  - Main content: mortality by vaccine-related causes over time.
  - Sample size: Not specified in paper excerpt.
- Gavi funding records.
  - Main content: cumulative support exceeding US$16 billion since 1999.
  - Role: treatment timing and program intensity context.

## Experimental Procedure
- Construct a panel with differential Gavi support timing across countries and vaccines.
- Estimate treatment effects using a triple-difference strategy with high-dimensional fixed effects.
- Validate robustness to heterogeneous treatment timing bias using modern staggered-treatment estimators.
- Perform event-study analyses to test for pre-trend violations.
- Estimate effects separately for broader vaccine set and newer vaccine classes.
- Translate estimated mortality effects into cost-effectiveness (lives saved and cost per life saved).

## Key Biology Insights
- Not specified in paper; this is a policy-econometrics study rather than a biological mechanism study.

## Implications
- Strong evidence supports vaccine aid as a high-impact and cost-effective global health investment.
- Results justify continued financing for large-scale immunization support in low-resource settings.
- Findings are relevant for current vaccine financing decisions, including programs involving newly introduced vaccines.
