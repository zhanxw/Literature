## Keywords

Gut microbiota; low-protein diet; white adipose tissue; beige adipocytes; bile acids; FXR; ammonia; FGF21; sympathetic innervation; gnotobiotic mice.

## Main Idea

Low-protein diets (LPDs) induce beige-adipocyte formation in inguinal white adipose tissue (iWAT) through specific gut-microbiota functions. The microbiota supplies two complementary signals: microbial bile-acid modification activates FXR in adipose stem/progenitor cells, while nrfA-dependent ammonia production increases hepatic FGF21. These non-redundant pathways converge on sympathetic remodeling and β3-adrenergic signaling; disrupting either pathway markedly reduces browning.

## Evidence Supporting the Main Idea

- In SPF mice, reducing dietary protein to 7% or less induced Ucp1, Elovl3 and Cox7a1 in iWAT within two weeks, with effects comparable to cold exposure or a β3-adrenergic agonist (Fig. 1 and Extended Data Fig. 1).
- The response was strongly reduced in germ-free (GF) mice and after antibiotic treatment. LPD-fed GF mice lacked the robust beige-cell morphology, transcriptional program and weight suppression seen in SPF mice (Fig. 1g–k).
- LPD increased circulating unconjugated bile acids and microbial dehydroxylation products. FXR-deficient or adipocyte-/Dpp4+ progenitor-cell-specific FXR-deficient mice showed reduced browning, whereas TGR5-deficient mice did not (Fig. 2a–g).
- LPD induced hepatic Fgf21 and circulating FGF21 in SPF but not GF mice. Fgf21-null mice phenocopied the impaired browning of GF mice, while recombinant FGF21 alone was insufficient in control-diet-fed GF mice (Fig. 2h–l).
- Defined mouse-derived consortia (20 strains, later reduced to a five-strain consortium) and a human-derived 19-strain/hu4 consortium transferred browning capacity to GF mice, but only with LPD feeding (Figs. 3–5).
- Human-derived consortia containing nrfA-positive Bilophila and bile-acid-modifying strains induced portal-vein ammonia, hepatic Fgf21 and iWAT browning. Deleting nrfA from Bilophila abolished these effects; ammonium chloride rescued them (Fig. 6).

## Main Novelty

The study identifies a mechanistic diet–microbiota–host circuit in which protein scarcity is interpreted by defined microbial functions rather than by microbiota presence alone. It links two distinct microbial outputs—bile-acid remodeling and ammonia production—to separate host tissues and demonstrates their joint necessity for beige-fat induction. It also isolates mouse- and human-derived bacterial communities capable of transferring the phenotype to gnotobiotic mice.

## Datasets Used for Evaluation

- **Mouse physiology and molecular assays:** SPF, GF and gnotobiotic C57BL/6 mice (generally male, eight weeks old) fed control diets or a 7% LPD for about 3–8 weeks; evaluated by body composition, glucose tolerance, iWAT mass and morphology, qPCR, histology, immunostaining, plasma metabolites and hormone measurements. Exact sample sizes varied by experiment and are shown in the figures, typically n=3–16 per group.
- **Mouse-derived microbiota:** Small-intestinal or faecal contents from LPD-fed SPF mice; 20 isolated strains from mouse B28-1 and reduced five-/seven-strain consortia were tested for transfer of browning to GF recipients (Fig. 3).
- **Human donor cohort:** Faecal samples from 25 healthy male volunteers aged 20–47 years. Supraclavicular brown/beige-fat activity was assessed by cold-stimulated 18F-FDG PET; donors with high, intermediate or absent FDG accumulation supplied microbiota for GF-mouse transplantation (Fig. 4 and Methods).
- **Human-derived bacterial isolates:** 33 strains from T19 recipients and five candidate “other” strains were narrowed to a four-strain hu4 consortium; permutation, colonization and metabolic assays identified required members (Figs. 4–6).
- **Transcriptomic datasets:** Bulk RNA-seq and single-nucleus RNA-seq of iWAT and liver from GF/SPF mice under control or LPD conditions; metatranscriptomics of gnotobiotic caecal microbiota. Sequencing data are deposited under DDBJ BioProjects PRJDB19530 and PRJDB19694.
- **Metabolomics:** Targeted and untargeted LC–MS analyses of plasma, ileal contents and bacterial cultures measured bile acids and related metabolites; ammonia was measured in portal and peripheral blood and bacterial supernatants.

## Experimental Procedure

- Formulate isocaloric diets with graded protein or amino-acid content and feed mice for up to eight weeks; compare LPD effects with cold exposure and β3-adrenergic stimulation.
- Measure iWAT browning using Ucp1, Elovl3 and Cox7a1 expression, histology, RNA-seq, tissue mass and whole-mount UCP1/tyrosine-hydroxylase imaging.
- Compare SPF, GF, antibiotic-treated, immune-deficient and receptor/gene knockout mice to test microbiota dependence and host signaling requirements.
- Profile bile acids and liver transcriptional responses by LC–MS, qPCR, bulk RNA-seq and single-nucleus RNA-seq; use tissue-specific FXR deletion and Fgf21-null mice to localize causal pathways.
- Transfer mouse or human microbiota into GF mice, isolate strains, construct defined consortia and use omission/permutation experiments to identify minimal functional communities.
- Test microbial mechanisms with in vitro bile-acid conversion, ammonia production, genome/metatranscriptome analysis, tungsten inhibition of formate dehydrogenase, Bilophila ΔnrfA mutants and ammonium-chloride rescue.
- Assess pathway convergence by deleting β1/β2/β3 adrenergic receptors, imaging sympathetic innervation and applying a β3-adrenergic agonist.

## Key Biology Insights

- Protein restriction, especially concurrent essential-amino-acid limitation, is a potent and reversible inducer of iWAT beiging; the response varies with sex, age, adipose depot and dietary context.
- Microbiota-conditioned bile acids act through FXR in adipose progenitor populations, with pseudotime analysis consistent with progenitor-to-beige-cell differentiation.
- Microbial nrfA converts nitrite to ammonia; portal ammonia selectively stimulates hepatocyte FGF21, providing a nitrogen-conservation signal during protein scarcity.
- FXR and FGF21 are parallel and non-redundant: either pathway alone is insufficient, and both promote sympathetic innervation and β3-adrenergic signaling.
- The bacterial taxa are not interchangeable by membership alone; successful consortia encode complementary bile-acid-modifying and ammonia-producing capabilities.

## Implications

The work suggests that diet-responsive microbial functions could be developed to amplify metabolic effects of protein restriction or other nutritional interventions. However, the experiments are primarily in mice, and the human donors were used as microbiota sources rather than as an intervention cohort. The authors emphasize unresolved questions about microbial sensing of low protein, systemic bile-acid routing, FXR-driven progenitor differentiation and the quantitative contribution of UCP1-dependent versus UCP1-independent FGF21 effects. Ucp1 mRNA is dynamic and does not by itself establish active thermogenesis, so translation to human obesity or metabolic disease requires further validation.
